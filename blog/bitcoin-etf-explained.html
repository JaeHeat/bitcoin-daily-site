Bitcoin ETF Explained: How Spot ETFs Actually Work

By Josh Molnar · October 2026 · 5 min read

In January 2024, the SEC approved 11 spot Bitcoin ETFs for the first time. Within two years, those funds pulled in over $57 billion of net new money and now hold roughly 1.29 million BTC, worth about $106 billion. That is more Bitcoin than any single company, government, or whale on the planet.

If you have heard the term thrown around and never quite understood what a Bitcoin ETF actually is, this is the plain English version. No finance degree required.

What is a Bitcoin ETF?

An ETF is a fund that trades on the stock market like a regular stock. A Bitcoin ETF is one of those funds, except instead of holding shares of Apple or Google, it holds Bitcoin.

You buy a share of the ETF through your normal brokerage account (the same one you would use for any stock). The fund buys real Bitcoin and locks it in secure storage. When you sell the share, you get cash back. You never touch Bitcoin directly, never need a wallet, and never worry about losing a password.

Think of it like buying gold through a fund. You own a slice of the gold sitting in the vault, but you never carry a bar home.

Spot vs. futures: the difference matters

Before January 2024, the only Bitcoin ETFs in the US held futures contracts, basically bets on where the price would go next month. Those futures ETFs had a nasty habit of drifting away from the real Bitcoin price over time, which meant your returns quietly leaked away even when Bitcoin went up.

Spot ETFs fixed that. A spot ETF holds actual Bitcoin, coin for coin. When you buy $1,000 of a spot ETF, roughly $1,000 of real Bitcoin sits in the vault behind it. The price tracks much tighter because there is no middleman contract expiring every month.

Who runs them and what do they charge?

The biggest names in traditional finance jumped in. BlackRock (ticker IBIT) leads the pack with about 62% of all the Bitcoin held across spot ETFs, roughly 806,000 BTC. Fidelity (FBTC) is the second largest.

Fees are remarkably low. Both IBIT and FBTC charge 0.25% per year. On a $10,000 investment, that is $25 a year. Compare that to the trading fees, withdrawal fees, and spread you would pay buying Bitcoin on most crypto exchanges, and for a lot of people the ETF is actually cheaper.

Why people buy them instead of just buying Bitcoin

  • Simplicity. No exchange account, no wallet, no seed phrase. It sits right next to your other stocks.
  • Tax accounts. You can hold a Bitcoin ETF inside an IRA or 401(k). Try doing that with Bitcoin on Coinbase.
  • Custody risk. The ETF provider stores the Bitcoin with institutional security. You do not have to worry about exchange hacks or losing access to your keys.
  • Regulation. ETFs file with the SEC. There is a paper trail and legal protections that do not exist on most crypto platforms.

The trade-offs you should know

Nothing is free. Here is what you give up.

  • You do not own Bitcoin. You own a share of a fund that owns Bitcoin. You cannot send it to a wallet, use it to pay for anything, or move it to another country in ten minutes. The whole point of Bitcoin being a bearer asset disappears.
  • Market hours. Bitcoin trades 24/7. The ETF only trades when the stock market is open, Monday through Friday. A weekend crash means you sit and watch until Monday morning.
  • Fees add up over time. 0.25% a year does not sound like much, but over 10 years on a growing investment it compounds. Holding your own Bitcoin costs zero per year forever.
  • Counterparty risk. You are trusting BlackRock, Fidelity, or whoever runs the fund to actually hold the Bitcoin and not mismanage it. That is a bet on institutions, which is the opposite of what Bitcoin was designed for.

So should you buy a Bitcoin ETF?

That depends on who you are.

If you already know how to buy and store Bitcoin yourself, the ETF adds a middleman you do not need. You are paying 0.25% a year for someone else to hold what you could hold for free.

If you want Bitcoin exposure inside a retirement account, or you never want to think about wallets and keys, the ETF is the simplest path. Just understand what you are giving up.

The $106 billion already sitting in these funds tells you the market has voted. Millions of people decided the convenience is worth the trade-off. Whether that is the right call for you comes down to one question: do you want to own Bitcoin, or do you want exposure to its price? Those are two different things.

Common questions about Bitcoin ETFs

What is a spot Bitcoin ETF?

A fund that holds real Bitcoin and trades on the stock market like a regular stock. You buy shares through your brokerage, and the fund buys and stores the actual coins behind them.

How much does a Bitcoin ETF cost?

The largest funds (IBIT, FBTC) charge 0.25% per year. On $10,000 invested, that is $25 a year in fees.

Can I hold a Bitcoin ETF in my IRA?

Yes. Because Bitcoin ETFs trade like normal stocks, they can sit in an IRA, 401(k), or any standard brokerage account. This is one of the biggest reasons people choose the ETF over buying Bitcoin directly.

Is a Bitcoin ETF safer than buying Bitcoin?

It removes the risk of losing your own keys or getting hacked on an exchange. But it adds a different risk: you are trusting an institution to hold your coins. Neither option is risk free.
