Can You Make a Living Trading Crypto? (The Honest Answer)

By Josh Molnar · October 2026 · 7 min read

The honest answer is yes. The more honest follow-up: the number of people who actually do it sustainably is far smaller than crypto social media would have you believe.

I have been trading Bitcoin full time since 2022. In the last 109 live trades, my win rate is 70%, my return on capital is 34%, and my worst loss has never exceeded 1% of my account. Those numbers let me trade for a living. They also took years of failure to build.

So yes, you can make a living trading crypto. The path there looks nothing like the highlight reels.

Can you make a living trading crypto? Start with the math

Before strategy or setups, do the math. Take your living expenses, divide by your account balance, and see what annual return you actually need.

If you need $60,000 a year and you have $50,000 to trade with, you need a 120% return. That is not a plan. That is gambling with a spreadsheet.

If you have $200,000 and you need $60,000, that is 30%. That is achievable with a real edge and real discipline. Most professional traders who survive long term are working in the 20 to 40% annual return range on meaningful capital. Not 500%. Not 10x in a month.

The single biggest reason most traders fail is not that they are bad at finding setups. It is that they start with $5,000 and try to make it their salary. The math never works. They have to take trades that are too big, one bad run wipes the account, and they are out of the game before they ever had a real chance.

The small-account phase you cannot skip

If your account is small, trading is not yet your job. It is your training program.

Here is how I think about the path. From a $500 account, the goal is to grow it to $5,000 using 1% risk per trade, not to pay rent from it. From $5,000, the goal is $20,000. From $20,000, you have enough capital to generate a real return, and by then you should have a system with enough live trades behind it to know whether your edge is real or imagined.

That process takes two to four years for most people who actually make it. If you skip the small-account phase, you skip the education, and you pay for it with real money instead of small money. This is not pessimism. It is just the math of learning a skill before you try to live off it.

Three things that actually separate full-time traders from everyone else

I have made most of the mistakes. Here is what I learned the expensive way.

Fixed risk per trade, no exceptions. I risk 1% per trade. Not 5%, not “it depends on the setup.” One percent, every trade, no discussion. This is the single rule that keeps traders alive long enough to find their edge. A 1% risk cap means I can lose 20 trades in a row and still have 80% of my account. That has never happened, but the rule means it never ends me even if it does.

Exits matter more than entries. I have tested this directly. Random entries, managed exits. The results were positive. Not because the entries were good, but because the exits were handled with a plan. Most traders obsess over when to get in. The ones who survive obsess over where they get out when they are wrong. If you want the full breakdown, the data on whether day trading Bitcoin is actually profitable covers how exits drive the numbers.

The journal is the job. I log every trade. Win or loss, I write down what I expected, what happened, and why. The journal is where patterns show up. It is where you find out your system works in trending markets and falls apart in chop, or that you close winners too fast on Fridays. Without the journal, you are flying blind. The data is sitting in your own trade history and you are not reading it.

What full-time trading in a bear market actually looks like

Most of 2025 and 2026, Bitcoin has been in a bear market. I am still trading. Here is what that looks like in practice: about two hours a day, roughly 18 trades a month, and skipping far more setups than I take. The discipline is not about working harder. It is about doing less.

The traders who tried to force trades every day to “make their month” are the ones who are not trading anymore. A bear market does not stop you from making money. It stops you from being careless with your money, which turns out to be the same thing.

The one thing that protects the account in every market environment is sizing correctly. If you have not thought carefully about how to size your crypto trades, that is the highest-leverage thing you can fix before anything else.

Making a living from crypto trading is real. It is just slower, more boring, and more system-dependent than anyone tells you on the way in.

Common questions

Can you actually make a living trading crypto?

Yes, but it requires either enough capital that a reasonable annual return covers your living costs, or years of growing a small account first. The traders who make it treat it as a business with strict risk rules, not a shortcut to wealth.

How much money do you need to make a living from crypto trading?

At a 30% annual return, you need around $200,000 to generate $60,000 per year. Trying to make a living from a $10,000 account requires returns that destroy most accounts before they ever get there.

What percentage of crypto traders are profitable?

Studies consistently put the long-term profitable rate below 20% for retail traders. Most losses come not from bad market analysis but from poor risk management and account sizes too small to survive a normal losing streak.

Is crypto trading sustainable as a full-time income?

It can be, with strict risk management, a proven and logged edge, and capital large enough that a livable return does not require oversized bets. Without those three things, it is not sustainable, it is just a slow account.

How long does it take to become a full-time crypto trader?

Most traders who make it take two to four years of consistent practice, starting small and treating early losses as the cost of a real education rather than a sign they should quit.
