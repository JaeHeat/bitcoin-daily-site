What Is a Satoshi? Bitcoin's Smallest Unit, Explained

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 5 min read

One Bitcoin costs about $58,200 this morning. Most people see that number and decide they showed up too late, or that Bitcoin is for rich people. Both ideas come from the same mistake. Nobody has to buy a whole one. Bitcoin is sold in tiny pieces, and the tiny piece has a name. So what is a satoshi, how much is one worth, and why does it change the math for a normal person?

What is a satoshi?

A satoshi is the smallest piece of a bitcoin that exists. One bitcoin is made of 100 million satoshis. Most people just call them sats.

Think of a dollar and a penny. A dollar splits into 100 pennies. A bitcoin splits into 100,000,000 sats. Written out as a number, one sat is 0.00000001 bitcoin. You cannot go smaller than that on the Bitcoin network.

The name comes from Satoshi Nakamoto, the unknown person who created Bitcoin. Satoshi did not pick the name. A user on an early Bitcoin forum suggested it in November 2010 for a bigger slice of a coin, and in February 2011 the name moved to the smallest unit. It stuck.

How much is one satoshi worth today?

With Bitcoin near $58,200, one satoshi is worth about six hundredths of one cent. That sounds like nothing, so flip it around and ask what your money buys.

  • $1 buys about 1,717 sats.
  • $10 buys about 17,170 sats.
  • $100 buys about 171,700 sats.
  • $1,000 buys about 1.7 million sats.

Going the other way, one million sats costs about $582 today. These numbers move every minute, because they are just the Bitcoin price cut into 100 million slices.

Here is a clean way to remember the scale. One satoshi would be worth one cent if one Bitcoin cost $1,000,000. That is math, not a forecast. Today we are a long way from it.

Why a satoshi matters more than the price of one coin

The $58,200 price tag does a strange thing to people. I have watched new buyers skip Bitcoin because one coin feels too expensive, then buy a coin that costs 50 cents because it feels cheap. They can own thousands of the cheap one, and owning thousands feels like winning.

It is a trick of the eyes. The price of one coin tells you nothing by itself. What matters is the price multiplied by how many coins exist. A 50 cent coin with 100 billion coins in existence is a $50 billion project. It is not cheap. It is just sliced thin.

Bitcoin is the opposite. It looks expensive because it is sliced thick. There will only ever be 21 million coins, and I broke down how many bitcoins there are right now in another post. Count in sats and the picture changes.

  • 21 million coins times 100 million sats is 2.1 quadrillion sats. That is 2,100,000,000,000,000.
  • Split evenly across roughly 8.2 billion people, that is about 256,000 sats each.
  • At today’s price, 256,000 sats costs about $149.

Read that last line again. An equal share of all the Bitcoin that will ever exist costs less than a pair of sneakers right now. Nobody is handing out equal shares, and millions of coins are lost or locked away for good. The point is the scale. The whole coin was never the entry ticket.

What sats do not change

I will not sell you false comfort, so here is the honest part.

  • Sats are not a discount. Buying $100 of sats is exactly the same as buying $100 of Bitcoin. If Bitcoin drops 20%, your sats are worth 20% less.
  • A small unit does not make a small risk. Bitcoin trades at less than half of its October 2025 high near $126,000. People who bought sats near the top are down just as much as people who bought whole coins.
  • More units is not more money. Owning 171,700 of something feels bigger than owning 0.0017 of something. It is the same $100.

What sats do change is the question you ask. Not “can I afford a Bitcoin?” but “how many dollars do I want to put in, and how often?” That second question has a real answer, and I worked through it in how much Bitcoin you should buy.

How to buy satoshis

You do not buy sats in a special place. Every major exchange and Bitcoin app already lets you type a dollar amount instead of a coin amount. Type $20 and you get $20 of Bitcoin, which today is about 34,000 sats. Many apps let you switch the display from BTC to sats so you see whole numbers instead of a long string of zeros.

A few things to check before you tap buy.

  1. The fee. A flat $1 fee on a $10 buy is 10% gone before you start. Small buys get hit hardest, so look at the fee as a percent.
  2. The minimum. Many platforms let you start with just a few dollars. Check before you sign up.
  3. The schedule. A small fixed buy every week takes the guessing out of it.

The takeaway

  • A satoshi is one hundred millionth of a bitcoin, the smallest piece there is.
  • One dollar buys about 1,717 sats today, and one million sats costs about $582.
  • The price of one whole coin is a bad way to judge whether something is cheap or expensive.
  • Sats make Bitcoin easy to buy in small amounts. They do not make it safer.

The people who say they missed Bitcoin are usually staring at the price of one coin. The coin was never the unit. The sat is.
