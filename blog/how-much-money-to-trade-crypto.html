How Much Money Do You Need to Trade Crypto?

By Josh Molnar · October 2026 · 5 min read

Most crypto exchanges will let you open an account with $10. That number does not answer the real question: how much money do you actually need to trade crypto? The exchange minimum and the amount you need to trade properly are very different things.

The real question is how much capital lets you size your risk correctly, survive a normal run of losses, and collect enough trades to know whether your system actually works. The answer is higher than most people expect, but lower than most people fear.

Why the minimum deposit is not the trading minimum

Exchanges set minimums to get accounts opened. They are not sizing advice. A $10 account means you are putting $10 into a market that can move 10% against you in an hour. At $10 total capital, 1% risk per trade is $0.10. That is not a trade. That is noise. Fees alone would eat it.

Sound trading means risking a small, fixed percentage of your account on every trade. That percentage needs to produce a position large enough to matter and small enough that a losing run does not end your account. The minimum that makes that math work sits well above any exchange minimum.

How much money you need to trade crypto with real risk management

The percentage that works is 1% risk per trade. If your account is $1,000, you are risking $10 on each trade. If your stop is $100 away from your entry, you buy $100 worth of the asset so that a $100 move against you equals your $10 cap. If your stop is $50 away, you buy $200 worth. The position size adjusts to hit the same dollar risk every time.

Here is how the math looks at different starting amounts:

  • $100 account: 1% risk = $1 per trade. Fees on most platforms eat most of that. Not workable.
  • $500 account: 1% risk = $5 per trade. Small, but the math functions correctly.
  • $1,000 account: 1% risk = $10 per trade. Enough room to size positions correctly on most setups.
  • $2,000 to $5,000: 1% risk = $20 to $50 per trade. Comfortable for active trading without fighting platform minimums.

The honest practical minimum is around $500. Below that, fees become too large a portion of every trade and position sizing gets too constrained to execute cleanly.

The percentage matters more than the starting amount

Two traders. One starts with $500. One starts with $50,000. They both trade the same system at 1% risk per trade.

Their results in percentage terms are identical. The $500 trader finishes with 34% more than they started. The $50,000 trader also finishes with 34% more than they started. The number of dollars is different. The habit, the discipline, and the edge behind that number are the same.

Across 109 live trades at 1% risk, the system that produced those numbers ran at 70% wins and a final result of +34% ROI. That percentage is account-size independent. The system does not know how big your account is. It only knows whether you followed the rules.

Why starting small forces the right habits

A trader with a $500 account feels every $5 decision. That is not a disadvantage. That is the curriculum.

Larger accounts can hide bad habits for a long time. A trader who over-sizes, skips stops, or chases losses might not see the damage at a $50,000 account for months. At a $500 account, the damage shows up fast and forces a correction before it becomes permanent.

The goal with a small account is not to get rich quickly. The goal is to prove the system works consistently before you scale it. That proof requires time and trade count, not capital. Traders who skip this step and jump straight to a large account tend to lose the large account the same way they would have lost the small one.

The honest answer to how much you need

$500 is the floor. It lets you size correctly, absorb a normal losing run without breaking, and gather enough trades to see a real pattern emerge. $1,000 to $2,000 is more comfortable, with less friction from fees and more flexibility in how you size each trade.

The more important number is the one you can afford to lose entirely. That is not pessimism. It is the one rule that separates traders who last from traders who quit after their first bad month. Trade with money where a complete loss would sting but not wreck your life. That floor, wherever it lands for you, is your actual minimum.

Common questions

How much money do you need to start trading crypto?

Around $500 is the practical minimum. Below that, platform fees take up too large a share of each trade and position sizing gets too constrained to work correctly.

Can you trade crypto with $100?

Technically yes, but at $100 with 1% risk you are only risking $1 per trade. Fees on most exchanges eat most of that, making it unworkable. $500 is a more realistic floor.

How much should I invest in crypto to start trading?

Invest only what you could lose entirely without affecting your life. The starting amount matters less than sizing each trade at 1% of your account and keeping that rule consistent.

Can you grow a small crypto account?

Yes. The percentage returns from a consistent system are the same whether you start with $500 or $50,000. The goal with a small account is to prove the system works before you scale it up.

How much of my savings should I use to trade crypto?

Only use money you could lose entirely without it changing your daily life. Active trading capital should stay separate from emergency savings and long-term investment funds.
