Bitcoin CME Gap: Do Gaps Always Fill? (440 Weekends Tested)

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 5 min read

Ask any crypto trader about the Bitcoin CME gap and you will hear the same line. Gaps always get filled. It gets repeated so often that people place real trades on it every Monday morning. I tested it on 440 weekends of price data. The gap does get filled most of the time. So does a line I made up.

What is the Bitcoin CME gap?

The CME is a big, regulated exchange in Chicago where funds and banks trade Bitcoin futures. A future is a contract that follows the price of Bitcoin. From its launch in December 2017 until May 2026, that market closed every Friday at 5pm New York time and opened again on Sunday at 6pm.

Bitcoin itself never closes. So when Bitcoin moved over the weekend, the CME chart reopened at a different price than it closed at. That blank space on the chart is the gap. Filling the gap means price travels back to where the CME closed on Friday.

How I tested whether CME gaps always fill

I took hourly Bitcoin prices from December 2017 to May 2026. For every weekend I marked two prices. The price at the Friday close, and the price at the Sunday reopen.

  • That gave me 440 weekends. The typical one moved price about 1.2%.
  • The biggest was New Year’s weekend 2021. Bitcoin went from about $29,200 to about $33,100, a jump of nearly 14% while the CME was shut.
  • I kept the 319 weekends where the gap was at least half a percent. Anything smaller is hard to even see on a chart.

One honest note. I used the regular Bitcoin price, not the futures contract itself. The two move together very closely, so the gaps are nearly the same size, but the exact dollar levels can differ a little.

How often does the Bitcoin CME gap fill?

Here is how many of those 319 gaps got filled, and how fast.

  • 44% filled within 24 hours of the reopen.
  • 72% filled before the next Friday close.
  • 85% filled within 30 days.
  • 96% filled at some point. That is 307 out of 319.

So “always” is false. Twelve gaps were still open when I ran this, and seven others took more than a year to fill. In March 2023, Bitcoin was near $20,100 on Friday afternoon and near $21,300 by Sunday evening. Price has not been back to that Friday level since.

Still, 96% sounds like a rule you could trade. This next test is why it is not.

The test nobody runs on the CME gap

Bitcoin moves a lot. It touches most nearby prices sooner or later, whether a gap sits there or not. So for every weekend I drew a second line. It sits the same distance from the Sunday reopen as the gap does, but on the opposite side. Nothing happened at that price. Nobody is watching it. It is a fake level.

Then I asked the same questions about the fake level.

  • Touched before the next Friday close? The real gap, 72%. The fake level, 75%.
  • Touched within 30 days? The real gap, 85%. The fake level, 87%.
  • Which one did price reach first? The gap came first 130 times. The fake level came first 185 times. Four times it was too close to call.

Read that last line again. Price reached the made-up level first about 6 times in 10. The gap is not a magnet. Bitcoin went back to it about as often as it went anywhere else that was equally far away. In the first day or two, the weekend move kept going more often than it turned around.

Are there still CME gaps in 2026?

Not new ones. On May 29, 2026, the CME switched its Bitcoin futures to trading around the clock, weekends included, apart from a short maintenance break. No weekend close means no new weekend gaps.

The old open gaps are still on the chart, and people still post them as targets. The numbers above are the full record of how those targets behaved for more than eight years.

Should you trade the CME gap fill?

If you have traded gap fills, that was not a dumb idea. The fills are loud and easy to remember. The misses are quiet. Here is what I would take from the data.

  1. Do not enter a trade only because a gap is open. Picture a trade with the gap as the target and a stop the same distance the other way. It would have been stopped out first about 6 times in 10.
  2. Treat the gap as one level, not a promise. It is a price a lot of people are looking at, the same as any other support or resistance level. It needs a real setup around it before it means anything.
  3. Keep the risk the same on every trade. A popular target does not earn a bigger bet. Decide where your stop goes before you decide where the profit goes.
  4. Test the rule before you trust it. Any rule with the word always in it deserves a fake level next to it. That is the heart of testing a strategy without fooling yourself.

The takeaway

  • The Bitcoin CME gap filled within a week 72% of the time and at some point 96% of the time. It never filled every time.
  • A made-up level the same distance away got touched just as often, and it got touched first more often.
  • Since May 2026 the CME trades Bitcoin all weekend, so no new weekend gaps are being made.

The gap gets filled because Bitcoin moves, not because the gap pulls it. Trade the setup in front of you, with a stop, and let the chart legends take care of themselves.

Common questions

What is a CME gap in Bitcoin?

It is the blank space left on the CME Bitcoin futures chart when that market closed for the weekend and Bitcoin kept moving. The gap is the difference between the Friday close and the Sunday reopen.

Do Bitcoin CME gaps always get filled?

No. Across 319 weekend gaps from December 2017 to May 2026, about 96% filled at some point. Twelve were still open and seven others took more than a year.

How long does it take for a CME gap to fill?

About 44% filled within 24 hours, 72% before the next Friday close and 85% within 30 days. The rest took months or years, or never filled.

Is trading the CME gap fill a good strategy?

Not on its own. A made-up level the same distance away on the other side got touched just as often, and price reached it first about 6 times in 10.

Are there still CME gaps now that the CME trades 24/7?

No new weekend gaps. The CME moved its Bitcoin futures to trading around the clock on May 29, 2026, so the market no longer closes for the weekend. Older open gaps still sit on the chart.
