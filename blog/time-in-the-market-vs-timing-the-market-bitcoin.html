Time in the Market vs Timing the Market: Bitcoin, Tested

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 5 min read

Ten years ago today, one Bitcoin cost about $737. If you put $10,000 in and did nothing, you would have about $819,000 this morning. Now take away just 30 days out of those ten years. Not 30 months. Thirty single days. Your $819,000 becomes about $15,500. That is the whole fight over time in the market vs timing the market, and I ran it on Bitcoin to see which side the numbers take.

Time in the market vs timing the market, in plain English

Time in the market means you buy and you sit. You do not try to guess the next move. Timing the market means you jump out when things look bad and jump back in when things look good. Timing sounds smarter. Who would not want to skip the crashes?

The catch is that jumping out means you can miss good days too. So the real question is simple. How many good days can you afford to miss?

What missing Bitcoin’s best days costs

I took every daily closing price from November 24, 2016 to November 24, 2026. That is 3,652 days. Then I removed the best days, one batch at a time, and checked what $10,000 turned into.

  • Held every day. About $819,000.
  • Missed the 10 best days. About $157,000.
  • Missed the 30 best days. About $15,500.
  • Missed the 50 best days. About $2,100.

Read the last line again. Miss 50 days out of 3,652 and you did not just make less. You lost most of your money, in an asset that went up 82 times. Fifty days is a little over 1% of the calendar. Almost the entire gain lived inside that 1%.

The single best day was July 20, 2017, when Bitcoin closed up 25%. Nobody sent a warning the night before.

The part the buy and hold crowd leaves out

Here is where most articles stop, because the story is neat. I kept going, because the same test works in reverse.

  • Missed the 10 worst days. About $5.7 million.
  • Missed the 10 best and the 10 worst. About $1.09 million.

So dodging the worst days helps even more than missing the best days hurts. Skip both and you still beat the person who held. On paper, that is a win for timing the market. I am not going to hide that from you.

The problem is not the math. The problem is where those days sit on the calendar.

Why timing the market is so hard in Bitcoin

I looked at the 30 best days and the 30 worst days and measured how far apart they were. They are neighbors.

  • 6 of the 30 best days came the very next day after one of the 30 worst days.
  • 14 of the 30 best days came within two weeks of one of the 30 worst days. If the days were scattered at random, you would expect about 5.

The clearest example is March 2020. On March 12, Bitcoin fell 37% in one day, the worst day in the whole test. The next day it closed up 13%. One week later it closed up 15%. Two of the best 30 days in a decade arrived within a week of the worst one.

It happened again on November 9, 2022, a 15% drop followed by an 11% jump the next day. And again on February 5, 2026, a 13% drop followed by an 11% jump the next day.

Think about what a timer has to do. To skip the worst day, you have to sell before it. To catch the best day, you have to buy back at the exact moment the chart looks its ugliest, often within 24 hours. Most people who sell on the worst day are not buying the next morning. They are waiting for things to calm down. By then the big day is gone. I walked through those moments in the history of every major Bitcoin crash.

What time in the market does not promise

I will not sell you false comfort. Holding is not painless and it is not a guarantee.

  • The person who turned $10,000 into $819,000 sat through that 37% day, and through every other day on the worst list.
  • Bitcoin today is about half of its October 2025 high. Holding through this stretch hurts, and the test does not say when it ends.
  • The giant days are getting rarer. 24 of the 30 best days happened between 2017 and 2020. Only 2 have happened since the start of 2023. Bitcoin is getting quieter as it grows, so the next ten years will not be a copy of the last ten.

What the test does say is narrower and more useful. Most of the reward showed up on a handful of days. Those days came mixed in with the worst ones. Anyone stepping out to avoid the pain was standing in the exact spot where the payoff landed.

The takeaway

  • Over ten years, 30 days out of 3,652 made the difference between $819,000 and $15,500.
  • Skipping the worst days would have been worth millions, but the best days and the worst days travel together.
  • You do not have to be all in or all out. Buying on a fixed schedule keeps you in the market without betting on one perfect day, and I tested that in DCA vs lump sum.

Timing the market does not fail because people are dumb. It fails because the day you most want to be out is usually sitting right next to the day you most need to be in.

Common questions

Is time in the market better than timing the market for Bitcoin?

Over the last 10 years, holding every day turned $10,000 into about $819,000. Missing only the 30 best days cut that to about $15,500, and those days came mixed in with the worst ones, which makes timing very hard to pull off.

What happens if you miss Bitcoin's 10 best days?

From November 2016 to November 2026, missing the 10 best days cut a $10,000 investment from about $819,000 to about $157,000. Missing the 50 best days left about $2,100.

What if you could avoid Bitcoin's worst days?

Missing the 10 worst days would have turned $10,000 into about $5.7 million. The catch is that 14 of the 30 best days came within two weeks of one of the 30 worst days, so dodging one usually means missing the other.

Why is it so hard to time the Bitcoin market?

The best days tend to arrive right after the worst days. 6 of the 30 best days in the last decade came the very next day after one of the 30 worst, when most sellers are still waiting for things to calm down.

Does holding Bitcoin guarantee a profit?

No. The holder in this test sat through a 37% one day drop, and Bitcoin today is about half of its October 2025 high. Past results do not promise future ones.
