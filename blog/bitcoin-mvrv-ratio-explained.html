Bitcoin MVRV Ratio, Explained (What It Really Tells You)

By Josh Molnar · August 2026 · 5 min read

There is one chart that answers the question every Bitcoin holder asks during a crash: is it actually cheap, or does it just feel cheap? That chart is the Bitcoin MVRV ratio. It takes two numbers, compares them, and gives you something price alone never can. A reading on whether the average holder is sitting on profit or sitting on pain.

What the Bitcoin MVRV ratio actually measures

MVRV stands for Market Value to Realized Value. That sounds complicated, but the idea is simple.

Market Value is just the current price of Bitcoin multiplied by the total supply. It is the number you see on every exchange and every headline. It tells you what the market thinks Bitcoin is worth right now.

Realized Value is different. Instead of pricing every coin at today’s price, it prices each coin at the price it last moved on the blockchain. If someone bought 1 BTC at $20,000 three years ago and has not moved it since, that coin counts at $20,000 in the realized value calculation, not at today’s price. Add up every coin at its last-moved price, and you get the realized cap.

The MVRV ratio is just market cap divided by realized cap. When it equals 1, the market price is exactly the average cost basis of all holders. Above 1, the average holder is in profit. Below 1, the average holder is underwater.

Why MVRV below 1 has flagged every major bottom

When MVRV drops below 1, it means the market is pricing Bitcoin below what the average person paid for it. Everyone, on average, is losing money. That is the zone where people give up, sell at a loss, and swear they will never touch crypto again.

It is also the zone where, historically, the smartest money has stepped in.

MVRV fell below 1 during the 2015 bear market bottom. It fell below 1 during the late-2018 capitulation. It briefly touched that level during the March 2020 COVID crash. And it spent about 47 days below 1 during the 2022 bear market, compared to 211 days in 2018 and 2019. Every single one of those moments was followed by a significant recovery. Not the next day. Not the next week. But within months, each of those readings preceded a major move higher.

That does not make it a crystal ball. But a gauge that has caught every major bottom in Bitcoin’s history deserves your attention.

What a high MVRV tells you

The opposite end matters just as much. When MVRV climbs well above 1, it means the average holder is sitting on big unrealized gains. Everyone feels like a genius. New money is pouring in at prices far above the cost basis of the people who have been holding for months or years.

Historically, MVRV readings above 3 have coincided with cycle tops. The 2017 peak, the 2021 double top. Those were moments when the average holder was up roughly 3x or more on paper. The temptation to sell becomes overwhelming, and eventually enough people act on it.

Think of it this way. MVRV below 1 is a market full of losers who want out. MVRV above 3 is a market full of winners looking for the exit. Both extremes resolve the same way: with a big move.

Where MVRV sits right now

As of August 2026, the MVRV ratio sits around 1.24. Bitcoin’s realized price, the average cost basis of all holders, is roughly $52,330. With Bitcoin trading near $78,800, the market is about 24% above that cost basis.

That is not extreme in either direction. The average holder is in profit, but not in the “take money off the table” zone. It is also not flashing the deep value signal that a reading below 1 would represent. It is the middle ground where patience matters more than action.

How to actually use MVRV (and how not to)

MVRV is a value gauge, not a timing tool. It tells you whether conditions are historically cheap or historically expensive. It does not tell you what price will do tomorrow or next week.

The biggest mistake people make with MVRV is treating it like a buy button. “It dropped below 1, time to go all in.” The problem is that MVRV can stay below 1 for months. In 2018 and 2019, it stayed there for over 200 days. Buying on the first cross below 1 means buying into a market that might still have months of pain ahead.

The better approach is to use MVRV as one piece of a bigger picture. When MVRV is below 1 and other signals line up, like the ones covered in how to spot a real Bitcoin bottom, the case gets stronger. No single indicator should carry the whole decision.

MVRV also pairs well with other on-chain gauges. Our Rainbow Chart explainer covers another popular tool for reading Bitcoin’s long-term valuation, and comparing several of them at once is far more useful than relying on any one in isolation.

The honest limits

MVRV is built on the blockchain’s record of when coins last moved. But “moved” does not always mean “sold.” Someone sending coins from one personal wallet to another registers as a move and resets that coin’s realized price, even though no actual sale happened. Exchange reshuffling, custodial transfers, and lost coins all add noise to the data.

The ratio also gets less dramatic over time. As Bitcoin matures and the holder base grows, the extremes in MVRV get smaller. The peaks are lower than they used to be. The bottoms spend less time below 1 than they did in earlier cycles. That does not make MVRV useless. It means the thresholds that mattered in 2015 might not be the exact same thresholds that matter in 2030. Watch the direction and the zone, not a single magic number.

Common questions

What is the Bitcoin MVRV ratio?

MVRV is Bitcoin’s market cap divided by its realized cap. It compares the current market price to the average price at which every coin last moved on the blockchain, showing whether the typical holder is in profit or at a loss.

What does an MVRV below 1 mean?

It means the market is pricing Bitcoin below the average cost basis of all holders. Historically, every time MVRV has dropped below 1 it has coincided with a major bear market bottom.

What is a good MVRV ratio for Bitcoin?

Below 1 has historically marked the best long-term buying opportunities. Readings above 3 have coincided with cycle tops. In between is a neutral zone where neither extreme signal is firing.

Is MVRV a buy signal?

MVRV is a value gauge, not a timing tool. A low reading means conditions are historically cheap, but the market can stay cheap for months. It works best as one input alongside other indicators, not as a standalone trigger.

What is Bitcoin’s realized price?

The realized price is the average cost basis of all Bitcoin holders based on the price each coin last moved on the blockchain. As of August 2026, it sits around $52,330.
