Bitcoin MVRV Ratio, Explained (What It Really Tells You)

By Josh Molnar · September 2026 · 5 min read

There is one number that tells you whether the average Bitcoin holder is winning or losing, and right now it is barely above the line that separates the two. The Bitcoin MVRV ratio just crossed back above 1.0 on September 20, with Bitcoin around $80,700. That crossing has only happened five times since 2012. Every single time, it marked the early stages of a new bull run.

That does not mean the next move is straight up. It means the math behind the market just shifted from pain to breakeven, and historically that shift has mattered more than most people realize.

What the Bitcoin MVRV ratio actually measures

MVRV stands for Market Value to Realized Value. Forget the acronym. Here is what it actually does.

Take every Bitcoin that exists. For each one, look at the price it was last moved at on the blockchain. Add them all up. That total is the realized value, the aggregate cost basis of the entire market. It is what holders actually paid, on average.

Now compare that to the current market cap (price times total supply). That ratio is the MVRV.

  • MVRV above 1.0 means the average holder is sitting on a gain. The market is in profit.
  • MVRV below 1.0 means the average holder is underwater. The market is in pain.

Think of it like checking whether the typical buyer is up or down on their purchase. When the whole market is underwater, sellers are exhausted. When everyone is deep in profit, euphoria runs the show.

Why MVRV matters at the extremes

The MVRV ratio has spent roughly 65% of Bitcoin’s trading history between 1.0 and 2.5. That is the normal range. The signal comes at the edges.

Below 1.0 means the average holder is losing money. People who bought higher have either sold at a loss already or are sitting on painful positions with no reason to sell except desperation. That is the environment where long-term bottoms have formed. The 2015, 2018, and 2022 bear market lows all printed with MVRV below 1.0. It is not a timing tool. It is a zone.

Above 3.5 is the other extreme. At that level, the average holder is sitting on more than a 250% gain. History says that is where selling pressure builds fast. The 2017 top and both 2021 peaks all printed MVRV above 3.5 before rolling over. When everyone is that far in profit, it does not take much fear to start a stampede for the exit.

Where the MVRV ratio sits right now

As of September 27, 2026, Bitcoin trades near $84,700 and the adjusted MVRV ratio sits around 1.02. The market barely crossed back into profit territory a week ago.

That is significant context. Bitcoin peaked near $126,000 in October 2025 and spent most of 2026 below the realized value line, meaning the average holder was underwater for months. Crossing back above 1.0 does not guarantee anything, but the pattern is hard to ignore. Every prior time this crossing happened, Bitcoin was higher 12 months later.

Does that make it a buy signal? Not on its own. It is one piece of the puzzle. Combine it with other cycle markers, like the ones covered in our capitulation guide and the rainbow chart breakdown, and the picture gets clearer.

How to use the MVRV ratio (without overthinking it)

The simplest way to read MVRV is as a temperature gauge, not a trade signal.

  • Below 1.0: the market is cold. Holders are hurting. This is historically where the best long-term buying opportunities have shown up.
  • Between 1.0 and 2.5: normal conditions. Not cheap, not overheated.
  • Above 3.5: the market is running hot. Profits are large and fragile. Caution pays.

The mistake most people make is treating it as a precise trigger. It is not. An MVRV of 0.9 does not mean the bottom is in tomorrow. An MVRV of 3.6 does not mean the top is today. But the zones have been remarkably consistent across four full cycles, which is more than most indicators can claim.

The honest limits

MVRV is a rear-view mirror. It tells you what the average holder paid, not what they will do next. It also treats every coin the same, whether it is a forgotten wallet from 2011 or an institution that bought last month. Lost coins drag the realized value lower, which can make the ratio look healthier than the real market feels.

The ceiling keeps falling, too. The 2013 top printed MVRV above 5. By 2021, the peaks barely hit 3.5. Each cycle, the euphoria zone shrinks because the cost basis keeps rising.

That does not make MVRV useless. It makes it a compass, not a GPS. Ask the right question, is the average holder in pain or in euphoria, and it is one of the most honest tools in crypto.

Common questions

What is the Bitcoin MVRV ratio?

It compares Bitcoin’s current market cap to the realized value, which is the total cost basis of every coin based on the price it last moved on-chain. Above 1.0 means the average holder is in profit. Below 1.0 means the average holder is underwater.

What is a good MVRV ratio for Bitcoin?

Below 1.0 has historically marked strong long-term buying zones. Between 1.0 and 2.5 is the normal range. Above 3.5 has signaled overheated conditions near cycle tops.

Is the MVRV ratio a buy signal?

Not by itself. It marks zones, not exact entries. An MVRV below 1.0 means the market is in pain, and every past bear market bottom formed in that zone, but the exact timing requires additional context.

What does an MVRV of 1.0 mean?

It means the average Bitcoin holder is exactly at breakeven. The market’s current value equals what everyone paid, in aggregate. Historically, crossing back above 1.0 after spending time below it has preceded major rallies.
