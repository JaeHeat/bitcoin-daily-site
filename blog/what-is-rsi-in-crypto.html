What Is RSI in Crypto? (And Why Most Traders Use It Backward)

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 5 min read

The setup I see again and again: someone spots RSI above 70 on Bitcoin, decides the market is “overbought,” and waits for a pullback. Bitcoin then runs another 30% while they sit on the sidelines.

Sound familiar? The RSI is one of the most popular tools in crypto. It is also one of the most misread. Getting it right does not require advanced math. It requires dropping one assumption almost everyone brings to it.

What Is RSI in Crypto Trading?

RSI stands for Relative Strength Index. It is a number between 0 and 100 that tells you how fast and how hard a price has been moving lately compared to the recent past.

Here is the plain version: picture a scale. Zero means the price has done nothing but fall over the last two weeks. 100 means the price has done nothing but rise. Everything in between sits somewhere on that spectrum.

The standard setup uses the last 14 candles. On a daily chart, that is the last 14 days of closes. On a 4-hour chart, it is roughly the last two and a half days. The RSI does not care about where price is in absolute terms. It only cares about how the last 14 moves compared to each other.

Right now, Bitcoin’s daily RSI is 38.6. That tells you sellers have had the upper hand over the last two weeks. It is consistent with the downtrend you can see just by looking at the chart. It is not automatically a buy signal.

The 70/30 Rule That Loses Money in Trends

Here is the assumption that trips most traders up. The standard teaching goes like this:

  • RSI above 70 means the market is overbought. Time to sell or wait.
  • RSI below 30 means the market is oversold. Time to buy.

This sounds logical. It falls apart in strong trends.

In a genuine bull market, RSI can sit above 70 for weeks or months without a meaningful pullback. During Bitcoin’s 2020 and 2021 runs, the daily RSI spent most of the time above 60. Traders who kept selling every time it crossed 70 missed most of the move. They were not wrong about the RSI reading. They were wrong about what that reading means.

The same trap runs in reverse. In a real downtrend, RSI can grind below 40 for months. “Oversold” on the daily chart can mean there is still a long way to fall. The RSI is not telling you a bottom is near. It is telling you selling has been stronger than buying lately. Those are two very different things.

The 50 Line: What Actually Holds Up

The most reliable use of RSI is not the 70 or 30 level. It is the 50 line.

RSI above 50 means up moves have been larger than down moves over the last 14 periods. It is a sign of strength. RSI below 50 means down moves have been larger. It is a sign of weakness.

That’s it. Think of 50 as the halfway point on a tug of war. Above it, buyers have been winning. Below it, sellers have been winning.

This does not tell you to buy or sell at a specific price. What it does is give you a quick read on who has had control lately. When you combine that with the direction of price on the chart, you get useful context. When RSI is above 50 and price is making higher highs, you are probably in an uptrend. When RSI is below 50 and price keeps making lower lows, you are probably in a downtrend. Neither is a surprise. But having both point the same direction gives you more confidence in what you are seeing.

RSI Divergence: When the Tool Gets Interesting

There is one advanced use of RSI worth knowing even as a beginner: divergence.

Divergence happens when price and RSI move in opposite directions. Here is what bullish divergence looks like in plain terms: price drops to $60,000 and RSI drops to 28. Then price drops again to $55,000, but RSI this time only falls to 35. Price made a lower low but RSI did not. That gap suggests the selling pressure is weakening even though price is still falling.

Bearish divergence is the mirror image. Price climbs to a new high but RSI makes a lower high than it did on the previous peak. The move is happening on less momentum than before.

Divergence is not a guaranteed reversal signal. No indicator is. What it does is give you a warning that the force behind a move may be fading. When you see divergence at a key price level, it is worth paying attention. When divergence shows up in the middle of a strong trend with no other reason to act, it is easy to fade too early and get run over.

What RSI Cannot Tell You

RSI measures the recent past. It does not predict the future.

It cannot tell you when a bottom is in. It cannot tell you a bull market is over just because it crosses 70. And it does not replace a trading plan with clear rules for entries, exits, and risk. Used alone, RSI will give you reasons to trade in every direction at every moment, which is a fast path to overtrading and losses.

The traders who get the most out of RSI use it as one piece of context alongside price levels, trend direction, and their actual edge. Not as a standalone trigger.

Common questions

What does RSI mean in crypto?

RSI (Relative Strength Index) measures how hard a price has been moving recently, on a scale from 0 to 100. A reading above 50 means buyers have had the upper hand over the last two weeks; below 50 means sellers have been winning.

What is a good RSI level for Bitcoin?

There is no single good RSI level. The 50 line is more useful than the 70 or 30 zones: RSI above 50 suggests recent strength, below 50 suggests recent weakness. Use it as context alongside the broader trend, not as a buy or sell trigger on its own.

Does RSI work in crypto?

RSI works best as a trend-direction tool rather than an overbought or oversold reversal trigger. In strong crypto trends, price can stay above 70 or below 30 for weeks, trapping traders who act on those levels alone.

What is RSI divergence in crypto?

RSI divergence happens when price makes a new low but RSI does not follow, or price makes a new high but RSI does not. It suggests the momentum behind the move may be weakening, even if the price has not reversed yet.

What does RSI below 30 mean in crypto?

RSI below 30 is called oversold, but in a genuine downtrend that reading can persist for weeks. It signals that selling has been strong recently, not that a bottom is guaranteed. Treat it as context, not as a buy signal on its own.
