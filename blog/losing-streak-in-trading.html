Losing Streak in Trading: How Many Losses Is Normal?

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 5 min read

Five losses in a row feels like proof that something is broken. Your system stopped working. The market changed. You lost your touch. Here is the uncomfortable part. A losing streak in trading is usually none of those things. It is math showing up on schedule, and almost nobody runs the math before it happens to them.

I ran it. The numbers changed how I look at every red week I have ever had.

How many losses in a row is normal in trading?

Start with a coin flip. Say you win exactly half your trades, and each trade is its own flip with no memory of the last one. Now take 100 trades. What are the odds that somewhere in those 100 you lose five in a row?

It feels like it should be rare. The real answer is 81%. Four times out of five, a perfectly fair coin-flip trader hits a five-loss streak inside 100 trades. Nothing broke. That is simply what random looks like.

Now make the trader better and run it again.

  • Win 5 out of 10. An 81% chance of five straight losses somewhere in the next 100 trades.
  • Win 6 out of 10. A 46% chance of five straight losses, and an 80% chance of four.
  • Win 7 out of 10. A 15% chance of five straight losses, a 43% chance of four, and an 86% chance of three.

Read that last line again. I win about 70% of my trades, with breakevens left out. That is a strong number. The math still says three losses in a row is close to guaranteed over my next 100 trades, and four in a row is nearly a coin flip. A good trader does not avoid the streak. A good trader just gets a shorter one.

Why a losing streak in trading feels worse than it is

Your brain is a pattern machine. Three losses in a row and it starts writing a story. The setup is dead. Someone is hunting your stops. You should try that other strategy you saw last night.

The story feels like analysis. It is usually just pain looking for a reason. And the two moves it pushes you toward are the two that do the real damage.

  • Switching systems in the middle of the streak. You drop a plan that was behaving exactly the way the math said it would, and you start a new one you understand even less.
  • Betting bigger to win it back. You raise your size at the exact moment you are most emotional. This is how a normal streak turns into a blown account.

There is a quieter trap too. After four losses, it feels like a win is due. It is not. The market does not keep score for you. Trade number five has the same odds as trade number one.

The number that decides if you survive a losing streak

You cannot control when the streak comes. You control what it costs. That comes down to one number, which is how much of your account you risk on each trade.

Take the same five losses in a row at three different sizes.

  • Risk 1% a trade. You are down about 5%. You need a 5% gain to get back. Annoying, and that is all.
  • Risk 5% a trade. You are down about 23%. You need a 29% gain to get back.
  • Risk 10% a trade. You are down about 41%. You need a 69% gain just to see your starting balance again.

Same trader. Same five losses. One shrugs and the other needs a small miracle. This is why I risk 1% a trade and never let a single loss grow past that. It is not caution for the sake of caution. It is the price of making a streak boring. I went deeper on the sizing math in the 1% rule for position sizing.

How to tell a normal losing streak from a broken system

Sometimes a strategy really does stop working. So how do you tell bad luck from a real problem? Ask three plain questions.

  1. Did I follow my rules on every losing trade? If yes, the losses are the cost of doing business. If no, the system is not the problem. The problem is that you stopped trading it.
  2. Is this streak longer than the math allows? If you win 6 out of 10, five straight losses is ordinary. Eight straight is rare, about a 4% chance over 100 trades. That is when harder questions are fair.
  3. Do I have enough trades to judge? Ten trades tell you almost nothing. A written record of every trade is the only way to know your real win rate, which is why a trading journal matters more than any indicator.

If you followed the rules, the streak fits the math, and the record is long enough to trust, keep going. If you cannot answer the questions at all, that is the real finding.

What to do during a losing streak

The rules for a red run should be short and boring on purpose.

  • Same size or smaller. Never bigger. Cutting your size in half for a few trades is fine if it keeps your head clear.
  • Same system. No new strategy until the streak is over and you can think straight.
  • Grade the trades, not the money. A loss that followed the plan is a good trade with a bad result. A win that broke the plan is the dangerous one.
  • Walk away when you are angry. The next setup will still be there tomorrow. Your account might not be if you chase it tonight.

The streak is coming. That is not a prediction. It is arithmetic. The only choice you get is whether it costs you 5% or your whole account, and you make that choice before the first loss, not after the fifth.

Common questions

How many losses in a row is normal in trading?

More than most people expect. A trader who wins half the time has an 81% chance of losing five in a row somewhere in 100 trades, and even a trader who wins 7 out of 10 has an 86% chance of losing three in a row.

Does a losing streak mean my trading strategy stopped working?

Usually not. If you followed your rules on every trade and the streak is a length the math expects for your win rate, it is ordinary bad luck. A streak far longer than the math allows is when harder questions are fair.

How do you survive a losing streak in trading?

Keep your risk per trade small. Five losses in a row at 1% risk costs about 5% of the account, while the same five losses at 10% risk costs about 41% and needs a 69% gain to recover.

Should I trade bigger to recover from a losing streak?

No. Raising your size when you are most emotional is how a normal streak becomes a blown account. Keep the same size or go smaller until your head is clear.

Is a win due after several losing trades?

No. Each trade has the same odds as the one before it, and the market does not keep score for you.
