How Long Should You Hold Bitcoin? (The 4-Year Rule)

By Josh Molnar · October 2026 · 5 min read

Here is a question that sounds simple but keeps people up at night: how long should you hold bitcoin? The internet will give you a hundred opinions. The data gives you one answer, and it is more convincing than any of them.

Since 2013, every single person who bought Bitcoin and held for at least four years ended up in profit. Not most of them. All of them. That includes the people who bought the exact top of 2017. The exact top of 2021. The worst possible timing, on the worst possible day, and they still came out ahead if they simply waited four years.

How long to hold bitcoin: the odds at every time horizon

Using every daily closing price since 2013 as a possible entry, here is the percentage of buyers who ended up in profit at each holding period:

  • 1 day: about 52%. Basically a coin flip.
  • 1 year: roughly 67%. Two out of three people are up.
  • 2 years: roughly 79%. The odds tilt heavily in your favor.
  • 3 years: about 99%. Nearly everyone who held three years made money.
  • 4 years: 100%. Not a single buyer, at any entry price, was still underwater after four full years.

Read that list again. The difference between a coin flip and a perfect record is simply time. Nothing else changes. The same volatile, scary, headline-driven asset that loses people money overnight has never lost a patient holder money over four years.

Why four years is the magic number

Four years is not random. Bitcoin operates on a roughly four-year cycle tied to the halving, the event that cuts the rate of new supply in half. Every four years, the amount of new Bitcoin entering the market drops by 50%. That supply shock has driven a new price high in every cycle so far.

So a four-year hold always captures at least one full cycle. You buy somewhere in the pain. You sit through the boring middle. And you come out the other side into a supply squeeze that has, historically, pushed the price well past whatever you paid.

This does not mean Bitcoin always goes up. It means that even the deepest crashes have recovered within one full cycle. The worst crash in Bitcoin history, a roughly 85% drop from peak, was fully recovered in less than four years.

The catch nobody talks about

Knowing the math is easy. Living through it is not.

Four years of holding sounds simple until your portfolio is down 50% and every headline says Bitcoin is finished. The people who bought at the 2021 peak watched their investment lose more than three quarters of its value before the recovery started. For over two years, every price check was a punch in the gut.

The 4-year rule does not say the ride is comfortable. It says the destination has been profitable. Every time. Those are two very different promises, and most people confuse them.

That confusion is why most people sell at the worst possible time. Not because the math changed, but because the pain got loud enough to drown it out. Check our live crash tracker if you want to see exactly where today sits compared to every past Bitcoin crash, day by day.

What about holding longer than four years?

The longer you hold, the better the median return gets. A five-year holder has never lost money either, and the average gain at five years is dramatically higher than at four. But the important threshold is four, because that is where the losing window closes to zero based on every historical entry.

There is one important caveat. Past performance does not guarantee future results, and Bitcoin is still a young asset. The sample size is about 13 years of daily data. But 13 years of daily entries is thousands of data points, and not a single four-year window produced a loss. That is not a guarantee. It is a track record that would be difficult to ignore.

So what should you actually do?

If you are buying Bitcoin with money you might need in the next year, you are gambling. The one-year odds are only 67%, which is better than a coin flip but far from safe. The asset can and does drop 30% or more in a matter of weeks.

If you are buying with money you can genuinely leave alone for four years, history is on your side. Every past buyer in that situation made money. The price you pay today matters less than whether you can survive the wait.

The real question is not when to buy. It is whether you have the stomach and the timeline to hold through what every bear market throws at you before the cycle turns.

The bottom line on how long to hold bitcoin

  • At one day, holding Bitcoin is a coin flip. At four years, it has a perfect historical record.
  • The four-year window captures one full halving cycle, which has driven a new all-time high every time so far.
  • The math is simple. The emotional cost of sitting through a 50% to 80% crash is not. Most people sell before the payoff.
  • Money you need within a year does not belong in Bitcoin. Money you can leave for four years has historically always come back with a profit.
