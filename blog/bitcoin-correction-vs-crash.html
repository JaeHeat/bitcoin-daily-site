Bitcoin Correction vs Crash: How to Tell the Difference

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 5 min read

Bitcoin correction vs crash, in plain English

Bitcoin is down about 40% from its all-time high near $126,000, set in October 2025. If you are watching that number and wondering whether this is a normal pullback or something worse, you are asking the right question. The difference between a bitcoin correction and a bitcoin crash comes down to three things: how far price drops, how fast it happens, and what is driving it.

Understanding that difference does not tell you what comes next. But it does tell you what has come next, every other time.

What counts as a correction

In traditional markets, a correction is a drop of 10% to 20% from a recent high. In Bitcoin, that definition is almost useless. A 15% pullback in Bitcoin can happen in a single weekend and mean nothing. The 2017 bull run had six separate drops of 30% to 40% on the way to nearly $20,000. Every one of them felt like the end. None of them were.

A more useful way to think about it: a correction is a drop that happens inside a larger trend. Price pulls back, shakes out the people who bought late, and then resumes the direction it was already going. The trend survives. The weak hands do not.

What counts as a crash

A crash is bigger, faster, and usually triggered by something structural breaking.

  • 2011: Bitcoin ran from $1 to $31, then fell 94% to $2. An exchange hack and thin liquidity did the damage.
  • 2013 to 2015: after hitting $1,150, Bitcoin lost 87% and took four full years to recover. China banning banks from touching Bitcoin was the trigger.
  • 2017 to 2018: from nearly $20,000 down to about $3,200. An 84% drop over roughly a year.
  • 2021 to 2022: from $69,000 to $15,500. The collapse of Luna, Three Arrows Capital, and FTX broke confidence in sequence. A 77% crash.

The pattern is not subtle. Crashes come with a narrative. Something breaks, trust evaporates, and the selling feeds on itself until the people who still believe are the only ones left. If you are watching the Fear and Greed Index, crashes tend to pin it below 10 for weeks. Right now it sits at 28, which is fear but not panic.

The one number worth tracking

Bitcoin has dropped 20% or more from a peak at least 14 times since 2013. Six of those drops cut the price in half or worse. And every single completed drop, all 14, was followed by a new all-time high.

That does not mean every drop will recover. Past results are not a promise. But the track record is worth stating clearly, because it is real and it is long. You can see every one of those drops compared side by side on our live Bitcoin crash chart.

How to read what you are in right now

Nobody rings a bell at the bottom. But there are a few honest questions you can ask.

  • Did something structural break? An exchange collapse, a major fraud, a government ban. Crashes need a catalyst. Corrections do not.
  • How long has it been? Corrections usually resolve in weeks to a couple of months. Crashes grind for six months to over a year.
  • Where is the fear? Mild fear (the 20 to 35 range on the index) is normal during a pullback. Extreme fear, single digits, is where historic bottoms cluster.
  • Is the broader cycle still intact? Bitcoin moves in roughly four-year cycles tied to the halving. A correction inside an ongoing cycle is very different from the unwind after a cycle peaks.

Right now, Bitcoin is about 40% below its all-time high and sitting just 3% above its long-term moving average. The Fear and Greed Index reads 28. That is uncomfortable, but it is a long way from the single-digit panic that has marked real cycle bottoms in the past.

What matters more than the label

Here is the part most articles skip. Whether you call this a correction or a crash does not change what you should do. If you have a plan, you follow it. If you do not have a plan, this is where the absence hurts.

The people who survived every past crash were not the ones who correctly named it in real time. They were the ones who either held through it with a long time horizon, or managed their risk tightly enough that the label did not matter. The drop is the same whether you call it a correction, a crash, or a Tuesday in Bitcoin.

The only question that matters is whether you sized your position so you can still think clearly while it happens.

Common questions

What is the difference between a bitcoin correction and a crash?

A correction is typically a 10% to 20% pullback inside a larger trend that resolves in weeks. A crash is a steeper drop, usually 50% or more, triggered by something structural breaking, and it can last six months to over a year.

How many times has bitcoin crashed more than 20%?

Bitcoin has dropped 20% or more from a peak at least 14 times since 2013. Six of those drops cut the price in half or worse, and every completed drop was followed by a new all-time high.

Is bitcoin crashing right now in 2026?

Bitcoin is about 40% below its October 2025 all-time high near $126,000. The Fear and Greed Index reads 28, which is fear but not the single-digit panic that has marked past cycle bottoms.

Does bitcoin always recover after a crash?

Every completed bitcoin crash so far has been followed by a new all-time high. That historical record is strong, but past results are not a guarantee of future recovery.
