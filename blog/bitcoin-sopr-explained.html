Bitcoin SOPR Explained: Reading When Sellers Give Up

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 5 min read

At every major Bitcoin bottom, one number has told the same story. The people selling were doing so at a loss. Not a paper loss on a screen. Real money, out the door, gone. And history says that moment tends to be very close to the floor. That number is called SOPR, and it is one of the most honest charts in Bitcoin.

What Is Bitcoin SOPR?

SOPR stands for Spent Output Profit Ratio. The name sounds technical. What it measures is not.

Every time Bitcoin moves from one wallet to another, the blockchain records the price at which those coins were previously acquired and the price they are moving for now. SOPR asks one simple question about those two numbers: did the seller make money, or lose money?

If a coin was bought at $30,000 and sold at $60,000, that sale contributes 2.0 to SOPR. The seller doubled their money. If a coin was bought at $80,000 and sold at $60,000, that sale contributes 0.75. The seller lost 25%.

Average that across every coin sold on a given day, and you get the daily SOPR reading. Above 1.0 means sellers made money on average. Below 1.0 means sellers took a loss on average.

What Happens When Bitcoin SOPR Goes Below 1

This is where the history gets interesting.

December 2018: Bitcoin hit its bear market low near $3,200. At the worst of the selling, SOPR fell to roughly 0.94. The average person selling Bitcoin that week took about a 6-cent loss on every dollar they had put in. That was the bottom of the cycle.

March 2020: The crash that became known as “Black Thursday” cut Bitcoin nearly in half in a single day. SOPR fell to around 0.95. Pure panic. Almost no one selling was making money. Within weeks, Bitcoin had recovered most of that loss.

November 2022: The FTX collapse wiped out confidence across the market. SOPR fell well below 1.0 during the worst of the selling. That was also the bottom.

The logic behind this pattern is simple. When sellers are taking losses, it means the people most willing to sell at any price have already sold. Once that group is gone, there is nobody left to push the price lower. That tends to be when the floor forms.

What SOPR Cannot Tell You

SOPR is a temperature reading, not a timer. It tells you whether the conditions for a bottom are present. It does not tell you exactly when the low will form or how deep it might go first.

In 2022, SOPR stayed below 1.0 for extended stretches before the final low was confirmed. A single dip below 1.0 is worth noting, not a reason to put everything in at once.

SOPR also works best alongside other on-chain tools. The MVRV ratio measures how far Bitcoin’s price is from the average cost of everyone holding it. The NUPL indicator shows total unrealized profit and loss across the entire network. When several of these tools point toward the same reading, the signal is stronger than any single chart alone.

Where Bitcoin SOPR Stands in Late 2026

As of November 2026, SOPR has been hovering near 1.0, with recent readings in the 0.99 to 1.00 range. That means the average seller today is roughly breaking even. Not the deep loss-selling that marked the floor in prior cycles. Not the confident profit-taking you see in bull markets either.

What to watch: a sustained move below 0.95 that holds for days or weeks. That is the zone where history says the last sellers are washing out. It has appeared at or near every major floor Bitcoin has printed.

How to Read SOPR Without Overthinking It

You do not need to track SOPR every day. The signal is clearest at extremes. When the market is crashing and headlines are calling the end of Bitcoin, pull up the SOPR chart. If it is below 1.0 and has been there for a sustained stretch, that tells you the people who panicked have already sold. The ones still holding are the ones who chose to stay.

That group tends to look smart a year later.

SOPR will not give you the exact day or the exact price. No honest indicator does. What it will tell you is whether the market is behaving the way it has at every other bottom in Bitcoin’s history. And right now, that makes it one of the most important charts to have on your screen.

Common questions

What does it mean when Bitcoin SOPR is below 1?

It means the average person selling Bitcoin that day is selling for less than they paid. When enough sellers take losses for a sustained period, that has historically signaled capitulation and clustered near market bottoms.

What SOPR level has marked Bitcoin bottoms historically?

The December 2018 bottom saw SOPR around 0.94, and the March 2020 crash bottom saw it near 0.95. Any sustained move below 0.95 has historically appeared at or very close to a major Bitcoin floor.

How is Bitcoin SOPR different from NUPL?

NUPL measures the total unrealized profit or loss held by all Bitcoin investors at any moment. SOPR only measures the coins actually being sold each day, so it reflects what sellers are actively doing rather than what holders are sitting on.

Who created the Bitcoin SOPR indicator?

Renato Shirakashi introduced SOPR in a research paper published on April 25, 2019. Glassnode later added it to their on-chain analytics platform, where it became widely followed by cycle analysts.

Where can I track the Bitcoin SOPR chart?

Free SOPR charts are available on Glassnode Studio and CryptoQuant. Both show the current reading alongside the full history so you can compare where the market stands today versus past bottoms.
