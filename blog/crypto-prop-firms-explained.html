Crypto Prop Firms, Explained (Are They Actually Worth It?)

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 6 min read

The pitch is almost too good. Trade a $50,000 account. Keep 80% of your profits. Risk nothing of your own money. All you have to do is pass a quick test first.

That is the crypto prop firm model, and right now it is everywhere. New firms are launching every month. YouTube is full of “I passed my crypto prop firm challenge in 7 days” videos. The question worth asking before you spend $200 on an evaluation fee is what the numbers actually look like for the people on the other side.

What a crypto prop firm actually is

A crypto prop firm gives you access to a funded trading account. You trade their capital, follow their rules, and split the profits. If you lose too much, the account gets closed and you walk away with nothing, since it was never your money to begin with. No personal loss beyond the evaluation fee.

That last part is the appeal. Normal trading puts your own savings on the line. Prop trading, in theory, gives you a bigger account with capped personal risk. The firm eats the losses above what the rules allow. You collect the wins.

The business exists because of that evaluation fee. Most firms charge $100 to $500 to let you attempt the funded account challenge. When nine out of ten people fail, the fees add up fast.

How the evaluation works

Most crypto prop firm challenges follow a two-step structure:

  • Phase 1: Grow the account by 8 to 10% without letting it fall more than 10% in total or more than 5% in a single day.
  • Phase 2: Grow it another 5% under the same daily and total loss limits.
  • Funded stage: Trade the firm’s capital, same loss rules apply, and collect your cut of profits.

The profit split on funded accounts is generous on paper. Most firms offer 80 to 90% to the trader, with some advertising 100% once you hit certain milestones. Those numbers are real. The question is how many traders ever reach that stage.

The business model most people miss

Here is the thing most of the YouTube content skips: crypto prop firms make most of their money on evaluation fees, not on sharing profits with successful traders.

That is not a conspiracy theory. It is just math. When roughly 90% of people who attempt a challenge fail and pay again, the firm collects that fee each time. The 10% who pass and get funded are real, and those traders do get real payouts. But the firm’s revenue does not depend on those traders succeeding. It depends on the volume of people who try.

That matters for how you approach it. A firm can be completely legitimate and still have a business model that is indifferent to whether you personally make money. Their interest is in running a fair challenge that is hard enough to generate a high failure rate but not so unfair that the community notices and stops buying evaluations.

What the pass rate numbers look like

Industry pass rate data is scattered because firms report it differently, but the picture that emerges is consistent. Roughly 10% of traders who attempt a crypto prop firm evaluation pass and get funded. Only about 7% of everyone who starts an evaluation ever receives an actual payout. And among the traders who do get funded, around 70% blow their account within three months.

Put that together: out of 100 people who pay for an evaluation, about 7 get funded. Of those 7, roughly 2 will still be trading profitably after three months. The other 5 will hit their loss limits and lose access to the funded account.

That is not a reason to avoid them. It is a reason to know what you are walking into.

What actually predicts who makes it

The traders who pass evaluations and stay funded for the long run share one thing: they already have a system that works before they ever open an evaluation account. Not a system they are figuring out during the challenge. A system they can describe in one sentence, have traded for months, and trust well enough to execute under the extra pressure of a loss limit.

The evaluation does not teach you to trade. It tests whether you can already trade within constraints. That distinction matters, because a lot of people use the evaluation as motivation to “finally get serious” about building a strategy. That mindset gets expensive fast. You do not build an edge inside a timed challenge under a loss limit.

The other thing that separates funded traders is strict position sizing. The daily loss limit is typically 5%. One bad trade at 2% risk per position does not end the challenge. One trade at 10% risk does. The rules punish size more than anything else.

So are crypto prop firms worth it?

For a trader who already has a proven edge, a prop firm evaluation is a reasonable way to access more capital than they could otherwise afford. The 80 to 90% profit split is real. The risk is genuinely capped to the eval fee. If your system works at $5,000, it probably works at $50,000, and the upside is real.

For a trader who is still figuring out their approach, the evaluation fee is tuition for a lesson that could be learned cheaper. The challenge does not fix a strategy that does not yet work. It just costs money while you discover that.

The honest framework is this: treat the evaluation fee as a question you are paying to answer. The question is not “can I get funded?” It is “does my system hold up under strict rules?” If you have a real trading system you have already tested, that question is worth answering. If you do not, pay for the system first.

The 2 to 3% of funded traders who stay profitable long term are not more motivated than the 97%. They are more prepared before they start.

Common questions

What is a crypto prop firm?

A crypto prop firm gives traders access to a funded account in exchange for following strict loss rules and splitting the profits. The trader keeps 80 to 90% of gains and risks nothing beyond the evaluation fee.

What is the pass rate for crypto prop firm challenges?

Roughly 10% of traders pass crypto prop firm evaluations, and only about 7% ever receive a payout. Around 70% of traders who do get funded blow their account within three months.

How do crypto prop firms make money?

Mostly from evaluation fees. Since roughly 90% of traders fail each challenge, the fees from repeat attempts are the main revenue source, not profit splits from successful traders.

What do you need to pass a crypto prop firm challenge?

A tested trading system you already trust, with strict position sizing. The evaluation tests whether you can trade consistently within loss rules, it does not teach you how to trade.

Are crypto prop firms legit?

Legitimate firms do exist and do pay out funded traders. The model itself is real. The issue is not firm legitimacy but the low rate at which traders stay profitable after getting funded.
