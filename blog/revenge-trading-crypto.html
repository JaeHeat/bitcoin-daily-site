Revenge Trading: The Math Behind Winning It Back

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 5 min read

You take a loss. It stings. A voice says the fix is simple. Take the next trade a little bigger and you are back to even by lunch. That voice has a name. It is called revenge trading, and almost every trader has listened to it at least once. Here is the part nobody tells you. The voice is right most of the time. I ran the math, and that is exactly what makes it so dangerous.

What revenge trading actually is

Revenge trading is taking a trade to win back money you just lost, instead of taking it because your plan says so. The goal quietly changes. You stop asking whether this is a good setup. You start asking how fast you can erase the red number.

It usually shows up in three ways.

  • Bigger size. You bet more than normal so one win covers the loss.
  • Faster entries. You jump into a setup you would have skipped an hour ago.
  • No exit. You move or remove your stop because you cannot stand another loss today.

Crypto makes all three easier. The market never closes, borrowed money is one click away, and there is always another coin moving. Nothing forces you to take a break. You have to build the break yourself.

The math of revenge trading: doubling up after a loss

Let’s test the purest version of the idea. You have a $1,000 account and you normally risk $10 a trade. Every time you lose, you double the next bet so that one win gets everything back plus a little extra. To keep it simple, every trade wins or loses exactly the amount you risked.

Watch what a bad run does.

  1. Lose $10.
  2. Lose $20.
  3. Lose $40.
  4. Lose $80.
  5. Lose $160.
  6. Lose $320.

Six losses and $630 is gone. The next bet in the plan is $640. You only have $370 left. The plan needed one more step and the account could not pay for it.

Now compare that to doing nothing clever at all. Same six losses, same $10 risk every time. You are down $60. That is the whole difference. One trader is annoyed. The other needs to nearly triple what is left just to see $1,000 again.

Why revenge trading feels like it works

Here is the trap. Say your trades are a coin flip. Six losses in a row on any single run is rare. Doubling up rescues you 63 times out of 64. Every rescue feels like proof. You were down, you pressed, you got it back. Your brain files that under skill.

Now count the money. Each of those 63 rescues ends with you up $10. That is $630 in total. The one run that fails costs $630. Add them together and you get zero. All that stress bought you nothing. It only changed the shape of your results. Lots of small wins, then one loss that takes all of them back at once. Add trading fees and the total drops below zero.

And that one bad run is not as rare as it sounds once you trade for a while. I worked out the odds of hitting six losses in a row somewhere inside 100 trades.

  • Win 5 out of 10 and the chance is about 55%.
  • Win 6 out of 10 and the chance is about 21%.
  • Win 7 out of 10 and the chance is about 5%.

So a coin-flip trader who doubles up is more likely than not to meet the account-ending run within 100 trades. A good trader meets it later. Later is not never. I went through how common these runs are in how many losses in a row is normal, and the short version is that the streak always shows up eventually.

How to stop revenge trading

You will not out-think the urge in the moment. It shows up when you are angry, and angry people are bad at math. So the rules have to be written while you are calm.

  • Fix your size before the day starts. I risk 1% of my account on every trade, and my biggest loss on any single trade has stayed capped at that amount. The size does not change because the last trade lost. That is the whole point of the 1% rule.
  • Set a daily stop for yourself, not just for the trade. Pick a number of losses that ends your day. Two or three is plenty. When you hit it, the screen goes off.
  • Put time between a loss and the next click. Stand up. Walk around. If the setup is real, it will still make sense in thirty minutes.
  • Write down why you are taking the trade. One sentence in your trading journal before you enter. If the honest sentence is that you want your money back, you have your answer.
  • Never move a stop to avoid a loss. A small loss you accept is cheaper than a big one you refuse to look at.

The takeaway on revenge trading

The market does not know you lost. It does not owe you the money back, and the next trade has the same odds as the last one. A loss is a bill you already paid. Revenge trading is how you turn one small bill into the last one your account ever pays.

If you are reading this right after a rough day, you did not fail. You felt the same pull every trader feels. The traders who last are not the ones who never feel it. They are the ones who decided what to do about it before the loss happened.

Common questions

What is revenge trading?

Revenge trading is taking a trade to win back money you just lost instead of because your plan says so. It usually means bigger size, rushed entries, or no stop.

Why is revenge trading so dangerous?

Because it works most of the time. Doubling up after a loss rescues a coin-flip trader 63 times out of 64, and the one run that fails takes back every dollar the other 63 made.

How do I stop revenge trading in crypto?

Write the rules while you are calm. Keep the same size on every trade, set a number of losses that ends your day, and put time between a loss and your next click.

Does doubling your position after a loss work?

No. With a $1,000 account and a $10 starting risk, six losses in a row costs $630 and the seventh bet is more than the account has left.

How many losses should end my trading day?

Two or three is plenty for most traders. The exact number matters less than deciding it before the day starts and turning the screen off when you hit it.
