Bitcoin Returns by Year: The Full History

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 6 min read

Bitcoin returns by year tell a story that the price chart alone cannot. Zoom in on any single red candle and the whole thing looks like a disaster. Zoom out to the yearly scorecard and a very different picture shows up: Bitcoin has finished positive in 11 of its last 15 full calendar years. The bad years were brutal. And every single one of them set up what came next.

Bitcoin returns by year, 2011 to 2025

Here is every completed calendar year, rounded to the nearest percent:

  • 2011: +1,437%
  • 2012: +193%
  • 2013: +5,463%
  • 2014: -58%
  • 2015: +34%
  • 2016: +124%
  • 2017: +1,369%
  • 2018: -73%
  • 2019: +92%
  • 2020: +303%
  • 2021: +60%
  • 2022: -64%
  • 2023: +155%
  • 2024: +121%
  • 2025: -7%

Count the red years: 2014, 2018, 2022, 2025. Four out of fifteen. That is a 73% hit rate on positive years, and the median annual return across all fifteen is north of +100%.

The pattern hiding inside the worst years

Every crash year arrived after a cycle peak. And every crash year was followed, within 24 months, by a year that printed triple-digit gains.

  • 2014 lost 58%. The next three years (2015, 2016, 2017) gained +34%, +124%, and +1,369%.
  • 2018 lost 73%, the worst calendar year in Bitcoin’s history. The two years that followed gained +92% and +303%.
  • 2022 lost 64%. The next two years gained +155% and +121%.

The people who sold during the worst year consistently sold to the people who bought during the worst year. That is not opinion. It is the math, repeated four times in a row.

Where 2026 fits

Bitcoin opened 2026 near $87,500. As of mid-November it sits around $69,200, roughly 21% lower for the year and about 45% below its all-time high of ~$126,000 set in October 2025. The Fear and Greed Index reads 20, which is labeled “Extreme Fear.”

If you have been watching crypto for less than one full cycle, this feels like the end. If you have seen the table above, it looks like the same chapter the book has already written four times. That does not guarantee a fifth repeat. It does mean the current mood is not new, and the people who panicked in 2014, 2018, and 2022 all have something in common: they sold near the low to someone who held.

Why the yearly view matters more than the daily one

On any given day, Bitcoin can drop 5% or 10% and the headlines will say the sky is falling. On a yearly scale, those daily swings disappear into noise. The yearly scorecard shows something the daily chart hides: the crashes are getting smaller over time. The 2014 bear lost 85% peak to trough. The 2018 bear lost 84%. The 2022 bear lost 77%. Each cycle, the floor rises.

That shrinking pattern does not mean the next crash will be gentle. It means the asset is maturing. More buyers show up at lower prices than they used to, and they show up faster. You can see the same thing in the live Bitcoin crash chart, which tracks every major decline from the peak.

What a crash year actually looks like from inside

Numbers on a list look clean. Living through a crash year does not feel clean. In 2018, Bitcoin fell from nearly $20,000 to under $3,200. Social media declared it dead. Google searches for “is Bitcoin dead” hit record highs. Major media ran obituaries. Funds that bought the dip in January were down another 50% by December.

Two years later the price was above $29,000.

That gap between how a crash year feels and how it resolves is the entire edge of a long-term holder. The information is public. Sitting through it is the hard part.

The takeaway

Bitcoin’s yearly returns are wildly uneven, and that is exactly the point. The gains do not arrive in smooth, polite increments. They arrive in bursts, usually right after the years that scared the most people out. 11 out of 15 years positive. Every negative year followed by triple digits within two years. The price you pay for those gains is surviving the red years without flinching. Most people cannot do it, which is exactly why the returns exist.

Common questions

Common questions

What is Bitcoin’s best year ever?

2013, when Bitcoin gained roughly 5,463% in a single calendar year. The next best was 2017 at about +1,369%.

What is Bitcoin’s worst year ever?

2018, when Bitcoin lost about 73% of its value. The second worst was 2022 at roughly -64%. Both were followed by strong recovery years.

How many years has Bitcoin been positive?

11 out of 15 completed full calendar years (2011 through 2025) ended in the green. That is a 73% positive rate.

Does Bitcoin always recover after a crash year?

So far, yes. Every negative calendar year (2014, 2018, 2022, 2025) was followed by a triple-digit gain year within 24 months. Past results do not guarantee the pattern will repeat.

What is Bitcoin’s average annual return?

The median completed-year return is above +100%. The arithmetic average is much higher due to extreme early years like 2013, but the median gives a better sense of what a typical year looks like.
