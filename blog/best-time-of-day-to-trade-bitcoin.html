Best Time of Day to Trade Bitcoin (26,000 Hours Tested)

By Josh Molnar · November 2026 · 5 min read

Bitcoin never closes. No opening bell, no closing bell, no weekends off. So most people assume the best time of day to trade Bitcoin is whenever they happen to be awake. I tested that against 26,000 hours of real price data. The market is open all day. It is only busy for a small slice of it, and almost nobody plans their day around that slice.

What I measured to find the best time of day to trade Bitcoin

I took every single hour of Bitcoin trading from October 2023 to October 2026. That is three full years and a little over 26,000 hours. For each hour I asked two simple questions.

  • How far did price travel? The gap between the highest and lowest price inside that hour. A bigger gap means the market was actually moving.
  • How much money changed hands? More money means it is easier to get in and out at the price you wanted.

Then I lined up all 24 hours of the clock, in New York time, and compared them. A trader needs both things at once. Movement gives you something to catch. Money on both sides means you can catch it without getting a bad price.

The busiest hours for Bitcoin are the New York morning

One hour wins and it is not close. On weekdays, the hour that starts at 10am New York time moved price about 1.3% on average. The hour that starts at midnight moved it about 0.56%. Same coin, same day, and the morning hour travels more than twice as far.

The three hours from 9am to noon New York time make up one eighth of the clock. Here is what happens inside them.

  • They carry about 22% of all the money traded on a weekday. Nearly double their fair share.
  • They hold about 1 in 3 of the biggest hourly moves of the last three years. If the big moves were spread evenly, that window would get 1 in 8.
  • Price sets its low of the day in the 9am or 10am hour about three times as often as it does at 6am or 7am.

This lines up with a simple fact. The Bitcoin funds that launched in January 2024 only trade during New York business hours, and a lot of big American money sits down at its desk at the same time. When I run the same test on data going back to 2017, 10am is still the top hour, but the gap is smaller. The morning has always been the busy part. It has gotten busier.

The worst time to trade Bitcoin is when nothing is happening

The quiet zone runs from about 10pm to 8am New York time. Price still wiggles, but it travels roughly half as far per hour as it does in the morning rush, and fewer people are trading. That creates two problems. There is less to catch, and the moves that do show up are easier to fake, because it takes less money to push price around when the room is empty.

Weekends are the same story stretched across two days. The average weekend hour moved about 0.49%, against 0.74% for the average weekday hour. Weekdays also saw close to twice as much money traded per day. Only about 1 in 8 of the biggest hourly moves happened on a Saturday or Sunday, even though the weekend is more than a quarter of the week.

I want to be straight about the limit of this. Busy is not the same as easy. The New York morning is where the real moves are, and it is also where the fastest fake-outs are. If your stop is sloppy, the busiest hour will find it faster than any other. This data tells you when to show up. It does not tell you which way price goes.

How to build a trading day around the busy hours

Most people do the opposite of what the numbers say. They trade at night after work, in the quietest part of the clock, and then wonder why every setup goes nowhere. If that is you, you did not do anything dumb. Nobody tells you the market has a rush hour. Here is how I would use it.

  1. Pick one window and own it. If you can be at a screen from 9am to noon New York time, that is the richest three hours on the clock. This is why trading part time for about two hours a day can beat staring at charts for twelve.
  2. Can only trade at night? Change the style, not the clock. Short, fast trades need movement. Quiet hours do not have it. A slower timeframe lets you set an order with a stop and a target, then let the busy hours do the work while you are at your job.
  3. Treat quiet hours as planning time. Mark your levels, write the plan, set alerts. Boredom trades in a dead market are how overtrading starts.
  4. Keep the same risk every time. A busy hour is not a reason to bet bigger. Price travels further, so the same sloppy size hurts more.

The takeaway

  • Bitcoin trades 24 hours a day, but about 1 in 3 of its biggest hourly moves land between 9am and noon New York time.
  • Late night and weekends move far less, with much less money behind each move.
  • The clock tells you when the market is awake. It never tells you the direction. That part is still your job.

You do not need more screen time. You need the right three hours, a plan written before they start, and the discipline to close the laptop when they end.

Common questions

What is the best time of day to trade Bitcoin?

Over the last three years the busiest window was 9am to noon New York time on weekdays. Those three hours carried about 22% of the money traded and about 1 in 3 of the biggest hourly moves.

What is the worst time to trade Bitcoin?

The quiet zone runs from about 10pm to 8am New York time. Price travels roughly half as far per hour as in the morning rush and fewer people are trading, so there is less to catch and moves are easier to fake.

Is it a good idea to trade Bitcoin on weekends?

Weekends are quieter. The average weekend hour moved about 0.49% against 0.74% on weekdays, and only about 1 in 8 of the biggest hourly moves happened on a Saturday or Sunday.

Does Bitcoin trade 24 hours a day?

Yes. Bitcoin never closes, including nights, weekends and holidays. It is open all day but only busy for a small part of it.

Does the time of day tell you which way Bitcoin will move?

No. The clock shows when the market is most active, not the direction. The busy hours have the biggest real moves and the fastest fake-outs.
