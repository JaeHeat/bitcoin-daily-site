Bitcoin Squeeze Risk Gauge (Live)

How much fuel is in the tank for a liquidation cascade right now, in either direction. Two live scores, one for a long squeeze and one for a short squeeze.

Built from two public inputs. Open interest measures how much leverage exists, the fuel tank, and it dominates the score because in our study of 96 leverage flushes since 2020 the long squeezes fired with open interest at a median 95th percentile of its trailing year. The funding rate measures which side is crowded and paying for it. In a back-test the gauge sat at a median 75 out of 100 in the days before historical long squeezes against a base reading of 61, and flagged 8 of the 12 in its high zone. It reads conditions, not timing. A high score means the fuel exists, not that the match has been lit. Education, not financial advice.

A squeeze is what happens when too many traders lean on the same side with borrowed money and price moves against them. Their positions get liquidated, the forced closing pushes price further, which liquidates the next trader, and a chain reaction runs until the crowded side is cleared out. A long squeeze cascades down. A short squeeze rips up. The May 2021 crash and the moves after the FTX collapse were long squeezes. The 40% Bitcoin rally in the summer of 2026 began with a short squeeze that closed a quarter of all open positions in six days.

This gauge reads how much fuel exists for each kind of squeeze, using two ingredients. The first is open interest, the total size of all open leveraged positions, measured against its own past year. That is the tank, and it matters most. When we studied every major leverage flush since 2020, we found the long squeezes almost always fired with the tank nearly full, at a median 95th percentile, while a near-empty tank produced only rare and shallow flushes. The second ingredient is the funding rate, the fee that the crowded side of the market pays the other side. It tells you which direction the leverage is leaning. Crowded, paying longs raise the long-squeeze score. Crowded, paying shorts raise the short-squeeze score.

Read it as a fire marshal's report, not a fire alarm. A high score does not say a squeeze is coming today. It says the building is full of fuel and one spark, a news shock or a broken level, can start the cascade. A low score says the opposite, that leverage has already been cleared out and even bad news has less to feed on. In our back-test the gauge flagged 8 of the 12 major long squeezes in its high zone before they hit, and it never claims more precision than that. Use it alongside the funding and open interest context in the other charts on this site.
